Copy-paste guide for generating the 24 collector-progression medal images. Verified against the real catalogue
(`src/lib/data/progression/medalCatalogue.ts`), the real names/copy (`src/i18n/locales/es/progress.json`), and the
real rendering code (`src/components/core/MedalStage.tsx`, ADR 0036, `docs/design/visual-foundations.md` § Medal
rarity). No em dash appears anywhere in this file, including inside the prompts.

## 0. Series to shape mapping

Direction decided with the owner: medals are not round pins. Each medal is a **geometric medal-emblem**, and the
outer plate shape encodes which of the six album series the medal belongs to. The rarity halo around the plate is
still painted by the app, not by the artwork (see § 1).

| Series (`MEDAL_SERIES`) | Album name (`progress.json`) | Shape        | Why this shape                                                                                         |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `first-steps`           | Primeros pasos               | **Circle**   | The plainest geometry there is: day-one, no ornamentation yet.                                         |
| `the-wait`              | La espera                    | **Diamond**  | A rotated square reads as a clock face or a watch crystal, which fits a series entirely about waiting. |
| `the-display-case`      | La vitrina                   | **Pentagon** | A house-front / cabinet silhouette: a shape for a shelf, not a coin.                                   |
| `explorer`              | Explorador                   | **Shield**   | A traveling collector's crest, kept short and wide rather than tall (see the safe-zone rule below).    |
| `chronicler`            | Cronista                     | **Hexagon**  | Reads as a technical seal or a ledger stamp: the record-keeping series.                                |
| `secrets`               | Secretas                     | **Star**     | "Secret" and "shine" are the same instinct: a star is the fastest way to say both at once.             |

A seventh, reserved shape is used only for future event medals (§ 4): **octagon**, so an event piece is
recognizable on sight regardless of which series it is filed under.

## 1. Art direction

> **Superseded 2026-08-26**: rarity is no longer encoded by the drawing style. See the banner at the top of this
> file and `medal-catalogue-v2.md` § 2 and § 3.

**Style v3 (final), approved by owner 2026-08-24.** v1 read as western heraldic fantasy in practice ("more Harry
Potter than anime," in the owner's words). v2 fixed that by unifying all 24 medals under one glossy kawaii
anime-sticker treatment, which read as native to anime merch, but it undersold the difference between a common
medal and a rare one: every grade wore the same badge, only the ring color painted around it by the app changed.

v3 keeps every hard rule below and the same six-shape, geometric-plate system from § 0, but drops the single
shared style. Instead, **the rarity grade decides the medal's entire visual treatment**, the way a real trading
card line changes paper stock, printing technique and finish from the common run up to the signed insert. The
owner validated this direction with a side-by-side test plate: the same shape and the same subject (a star
holding a crescent moon with a small owl perched on its tip) rendered once per grade, all five approved as-is on
2026-08-24 (the first-print grade got one addition afterward, rule 12 below).

**Rarity to style mapping:**

| Rarity        | Style                                                  | What defines it                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `normal`      | 1990s shonen anime, classic action-manga TV adaptation | Bold dynamic thick outlines, exaggerated confident shapes, flat saturated retro palette (warm orange, deep red, mustard yellow, teal), hard-edged cel shadows with sharp shapes rather than soft gradients, a retro speed-line or impact-star accent behind the subject.                                                                            |
| `first-print` | Black-and-white manga ink                              | Pure ink linework, traditional screentone shading (dot patterns and cross-hatching for gradients and shadows), high-contrast black and white as the base, plus the small color accent required by rule 12 below.                                                                                                                                    |
| `limited`     | Retro 16-bit pixel art                                 | Console-sprite look: a low logical resolution (about 48 by 48 px) scaled up with hard crisp pixel edges and no anti-aliasing, a limited retro palette of about 8 to 12 colors, dithering instead of gradients.                                                                                                                                      |
| `holo`        | The `normal` shonen style, foiled                      | Exactly the `normal` base art (same palette, same outlines, same speed-line accent), with a prismatic holographic foil finish layered on top like a rare TCG holo card: an iridescent rainbow sheen sweeping diagonally across the plate, prismatic streaks, sparkle glints, refracted rainbow flares, without hiding the base linework or subject. |
| `signed`      | Dark seinen manga engraving                            | A worn, weathered dark-metal plate, dense fine crosshatched engraving linework, dramatic hard directional shadows, a moody near-monochrome palette (charcoal black, iron gray) with one warm antique-gold accent used sparingly on select engraved edges and highlights, an epic and prestigious feel befitting the rarest grade.                   |

**Hard rules, every medal, no exceptions:**

1. **Transparent background.** No canvas color, no scene, no drop shadow. PNG with an alpha channel.
2. **1024 by 1024 px canvas**, plate centered, upright, front-facing (no rotation, no 3/4 perspective).
3. **The plate and its motif fill about 80% of the canvas** and breathe: even margin on every side, nothing
   touching the edge.
4. **The shape must be the series' shape from § 0**, same orientation and proportion within that series (every
   diamond in "the wait" sits at the same angle and the same size relative to the canvas; every hexagon in
   "chronicler" does too). Do not reinterpret a series' shape medal to medal.
5. **Every point of the plate silhouette must stay inside a centered circle at about 85% of the canvas
   diameter.** This is not a stylistic choice: `MedalStage.tsx` renders the artwork inside a `rounded-full
overflow-hidden` mask (a literal CSS circular crop, see line ~112), so the app displays every medal, whatever
   its plate shape, through a circular window. A regular polygon (diamond, pentagon, hexagon, star) inscribed in
   a circle is naturally safe; the one shape that needs a deliberate adjustment is the shield (§ 0, Explorer):
   keep it **short and wide**, never a tall pointed shield, or its bottom tip will be cut off by the circular
   crop.
6. **No ring, no circular frame, no border beyond the plate's own rim.** The rarity ring (`--rarity-*`
   from ADR 0036 / `visual-foundations.md`) is painted by the app around the artwork slot at render time, in the
   grade's own color. Drawing a second ring, frame, or halo inside the art itself would visually collide with it.
7. **No text, no numbers, no watermark, no signature, no logo** anywhere in the image. Rarity is communicated
   entirely outside the artwork (the app's ring plus a mandatory text label, per ADR 0036 §3); numbering for a
   future limited/event medal is stamped by the app too, never baked into the art.
8. **No existing characters, franchises, trademarks, or copyrighted material of any kind.** This is a legal
   requirement, not a style preference: every motif in § 3 is generic (an hourglass, a moon, a shop door), never
   a reference to any specific show, game, or brand. If you see the image tool drift toward a recognizable
   character or logo, regenerate.
9. **Palette is set by the rarity grade, not by one shared v2 palette.** Use the grade's own palette from the
   table above, consistently across every medal that carries that grade. Never mix a different grade's palette
   into a medal: a `normal` medal never gets the seinen near-monochrome treatment, a `signed` medal never gets
   the bright shonen colors. The rarity ring still carries the rarity signal on its own (ADR 0036); the art's
   job is to make the family resemblance within a grade unmistakable.
10. **Legibility at small size.** The app's smallest render slot is 72 px (`MedalStage`'s `sm` size, used in
    toasts and dense rows); test every finished image by shrinking it to **32 px** in an image viewer as a
    deliberately conservative margin below that real minimum. If the plate shape or the motif turns into a
    blur or the shape reads as a generic blob, the linework is too thin or the motif too busy.
11. **Consistent treatment within a rarity grade, across all its medals.** It is expected, by design, that
    `normal`, `first-print`, `limited`, `holo`, and `signed` medals look meaningfully different from one
    another: that difference is the entire point of rule 9. What must stay constant is the treatment inside one
    grade: every `normal` medal keeps the same outline weight and palette, every `limited` medal keeps the same
    logical pixel resolution, every `first-print` medal keeps the same screentone density, and so on. If a
    generation looks off-family from the others sharing its grade, regenerate rather than accept it as "close
    enough."
12. **`first-print` and `signed` must each carry one small, distinctive color accent; neither ships as pure
    grayscale.** This is a legibility and confusion rule, not a taste one: `MedalStage` already renders any
    **locked** medal, of any grade, as a grayscale silhouette under a padlock overlay (a CSS filter applied at
    render time, see § 5). A genuinely colorless `first-print` medal art risks reading, at a glance, as that
    same locked-grayscale treatment even while unlocked. `signed` already satisfies this by design (the warm
    antique-gold accent from the table above); `first-print` needs a small flat spot-color accent of its own,
    already written into its base template in § 2. Note the one open discrepancy this rule creates: the
    first-print grade's approved test render is pure black-and-white with zero color, because this rule was
    added to the guide after that render was approved. Treat `first-print`'s look as final in every other
    respect, but run one fresh regenerate-and-approve pass on it once the accent is added, before batch
    -producing the five `first-print` medals in § 3b.

## 2. Base prompt templates by rarity

> **Superseded 2026-08-26**: the per-grade templates below belong to the style this guide's § 1 describes. The
> current generator is `art-drafts/medals-v2/build_prompts.py`.

Five reusable single-paragraph templates, one per rarity grade, each carrying two placeholders: `{SHAPE}` (the
series' shape from § 0, always phrased as "a plate cut into a {SHAPE} silhouette", the wording already proven
safe for every shape including the shield) and `{SUBJECT}` (the medal's visual concept, from § 3a). For the
Explorer series, `{SHAPE}` is the same "short, wide heraldic shield" phrase used throughout § 0's shield rule.
Paste the whole paragraph for a row's rarity into the image tool with both placeholders filled in; never send a
prompt with a placeholder still in curly braces.

**`normal`:**

```
Illustrate a single collectible medal-emblem badge in a 1990s shonen anime style reminiscent of classic action
manga TV adaptations: a plate cut into a {SHAPE} silhouette, holding a centered illustration of {SUBJECT}, drawn
with bold dynamic thick outlines and exaggerated confident shapes. Flat saturated retro color palette (warm
orange, deep red, mustard yellow, teal), hard-edged cel shadows with sharp shadow shapes rather than soft
gradients, an energetic retro speed-line or impact-star accent behind the subject for dynamic action-badge
energy. The plate and its motif are centered, upright and front-facing, filling about 80 percent of the canvas,
with every point of the {SHAPE} silhouette staying inside a centered circle at about 85 percent of the canvas
diameter. No outer ring, no circular frame, no text, no numbers, no watermark, no signature, no logo. No existing
characters, franchises, trademarks or copyrighted material of any kind, an original generic icon only.
Transparent background, no drop shadow, no background scenery. Square canvas, 1024 by 1024 pixels, PNG,
transparent alpha channel.
```

## 4. Event series (future, numbered medals)

## 5. Integration checklist

**Done, 2026-08-24.** All 24 final PNGs were resized to 512 by 512 (still comfortably above the largest
render slot, `s-2xl` at 262 px) and palette-quantized (256 colors, `Image.Quantize.FASTOCTREE`), which cut
the total payload from roughly 24.5 MB to about 1.3 MB (each file well under the 150 KB target, largest at
94 KB) with no visible quality loss. Files live in `public/medals/`, every row in
`src/lib/data/progression/medalCatalogue.ts` carries its `imageKey`, and the integration was verified live
against the owner's real dev data at `/progress/medals`, a medal detail page, and the dashboard's "Últimas
medallas" strip, in both light and dark theme: unlocked medals render in full color, locked medals render
as a grayscale silhouette of that same art under the padlock overlay, and the `first-print` grade's red
accent (rule 12) reads clearly against the locked-grayscale treatment at every render size. No code changes
were needed beyond the catalogue edit; `MedalStage`'s existing locked/unlocked handling worked as designed
against real artwork.

Once the image tool has produced the images:

1. **Verify each PNG** is 1024x1024, has a genuinely transparent background (check in an image viewer, not just
   "looks white"), matches its series shape (§ 0) with no clipped corners, and matches its rarity's style
   treatment (§ 1 table): a `normal` medal in the shonen look, `first-print` in black-and-white ink with its red
   accent, `limited` in pixel art, `holo` in shonen-plus-foil, `signed` in seinen engraving with its gold accent.
2. **Create the folder** if it does not exist yet: `public/medals/` (it does not exist in the repo today).
3. **Save each file** under the exact destination name from § 3a, for example `public/medals/first-order.png`.
